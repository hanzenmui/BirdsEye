## Finding 1: Ahaziah of Judah's death location is stated as "Beth-haggan" (in both his own description and Jehu's description), but the ESV text says he was shot near the ascent of Gur (by Ibleam) while fleeing *toward* Beth-haggan, and actually died at Megiddo

- **Category:** Incorrect
- **Verse(s):** 2 Kings 9:27 (ESV, live-fetched): "When Ahaziah the king of Judah saw this, he fled in the direction of Beth-haggan. And Jehu followed after him, saying, 'Shoot him also.' And they shot him in the chariot at the ascent of Gur, which is by Ibleam. And he fled to Megiddo and died there."
- **Current DB state:** `scripts/seed-2kings.ts` line 105 (`jehu.description`): "Killed Joram king of Israel and Ahaziah king of Judah **at Jezreel**." Line 170 (`ahaziah_judah.description`): "Went to visit the wounded Joram king of Israel at Jezreel and was caught in Jehu's revolution — **Jehu killed him at Beth-haggan** as he fled."
- **Why this is flagged:** Beth-haggan is only the *direction* Ahaziah fled toward, not the place he was killed — the text is explicit that he was shot near "the ascent of Gur, which is by Ibleam" and then continued fleeing to Megiddo, "and died there." Jehu's description compounds this by placing the killing "at Jezreel," which is where Joram (not Ahaziah) was killed; Ahaziah's flight and shooting began after he fled Jezreel, at a separate location entirely. Neither the two scripture refs (`jehu` and `ahaziah_judah`, both citing "2 Kings" ranges that include 9:27) state an incorrect location themselves — the note text for both refs is location-free ("kills Joram, Ahaziah, Jezebel; destroys Baal" and "walks in Ahab's ways; killed by Jehu" respectively) — so only the two `description` fields carry the error.
- **Proposed correction:** In `jehu.description`, change "Killed Joram king of Israel and Ahaziah king of Judah at Jezreel" to something like "Killed Joram king of Israel at Jezreel and Ahaziah king of Judah near Ibleam as he fled toward Beth-haggan." In `ahaziah_judah.description`, change "Jehu killed him at Beth-haggan as he fled" to "Jehu's men shot him near Ibleam as he fled toward Beth-haggan; he escaped to Megiddo and died there."
- **Severity:** Important — this is a specific, textually well-defined geographic/narrative detail (three distinct place names: Jezreel, the ascent of Gur/Ibleam, and Megiddo, each playing a different role) that the DB conflates into a single wrong location repeated in two people's descriptions.

## Finding 2: Jehoiada's description states as settled fact that he "lived to 130," but this detail is not present anywhere in 2 Kings — it appears only in 2 Chronicles 24:15 — while Jehoiada's own scripture refs cite only "2 Kings"

- **Category:** Unsupported
- **Verse(s):** 2 Chronicles 24:15 (ESV, live-fetched): "But Jehoiada grew old and full of days, and died. He was 130 years old at his death." No parallel statement of Jehoiada's age or lifespan exists anywhere in 2 Kings 11–12 (the chapters covering Jehoiada's story in Kings); 2 Kings never states his age or records his death at all.
- **Current DB state:** `scripts/seed-2kings.ts` line 121 (`jehoiada.description`): "...Guided the young king in reforming worship. **Lived to 130** and was buried among the kings because of all the good he had done for Israel. His son Zechariah was later killed by Joash." The person's only scripture ref (line 270): `insertRef("jehoiada", "2 Kings", 11, 4, 12, 16, ...)` — book is "2 Kings" only; no 2 Chronicles ref exists for Jehoiada at all.
- **Why this is flagged:** This is exactly the detail the task brief calls out as needing special scrutiny. The "130 years" figure and the burial-among-the-kings detail are both real and accurate, but they come exclusively from 2 Chronicles 24:15-16, a different book than the one cited for this person's entire scripture-ref record. As written, a reader following the `jehoiada` refs back to "2 Kings 11:4–12:16" would find no textual basis for "lived to 130" — the claim is unsupported by the cited source and only supported by an uncited cross-book text. (Compare: the DB does this correctly elsewhere for `zechariah_jehoiada`, whose entire ref is explicitly tagged as `"2 Chronicles"` even though the person appears in the middle of an otherwise 2-Kings-focused seed file — showing the seed file's own convention is to cite the book a detail actually comes from.)
- **Proposed correction:** Either (a) add a second scripture ref for `jehoiada` citing `"2 Chronicles", 24, 15, 24, 16` with a note like "Died at 130, full of days; buried among the kings for his good deeds," or (b) soften the description to attribute the detail explicitly, e.g. "Chronicles records he lived to 130 and was buried among the kings..." Given the seed file's established convention of adding a distinct ref when a detail comes from a different book (as done for Zechariah and for Manasseh's Chronicles-sourced captivity/repentance note in his own description), option (a) is the more consistent fix.
- **Severity:** Important — the fact itself is true and well-attested (just in the other book), but as currently structured the DB asserts a fact under a citation that doesn't support it, which is precisely the kind of sourcing gap this audit is meant to catch.

## Finding 3: Zechariah son of Jehoiada's description cites Matthew 23:35 as corroborating his identity, but that verse explicitly names the murdered Zechariah's father as "Barachiah," not Jehoiada — a real and long-recognized textual discrepancy, not a minor wording issue

- **Category:** Unsupported
- **Verse(s):** Matthew 23:35 (ESV, live-fetched): "so that on you may come all the righteous blood shed on earth, from the blood of righteous Abel to the blood of Zechariah the son of Barachiah, whom you murdered between the sanctuary and the altar." (ESV footnote: "Some manuscripts omit *the son of Barachiah*.") 2 Chronicles 24:20-21 (ESV, live-fetched) identifies the murdered Zechariah only as "the son of Jehoiada the priest" and gives no other genealogical name.
- **Current DB state:** `scripts/seed-2kings.ts` line 205 (`zechariah_jehoiada.description`): "...The people stoned him in the Temple court at the king's command. His dying words: 'May the Lord see this and call you to account.' **Jesus cited his blood among the righteous blood shed from Abel to Zechariah that would fall on that generation (Matt 23:35).** Not to be confused with the post-exilic prophet Zechariah son of Berechiah."
- **Why this is flagged:** This is a genuine, long-documented interpretive crux, not a trivial wording slip: Matthew 23:35 names the murdered Zechariah's father as "Barachiah" (Greek Barachias, i.e., Berechiah), which is the patronymic of the *different*, post-exilic prophet Zechariah (Zech 1:1, "Zechariah, the son of Berechiah, son of Iddo") — the very figure the DB's own description immediately says "not to be confused with." In other words, the description simultaneously (a) cites Matt 23:35 as proof text for the Jehoiada-era Zechariah, and (b) warns not to confuse this person with "the post-exilic prophet Zechariah son of Berechiah" — but Matt 23:35's own wording names the father as Berechiah/Barachiah, i.e., attaches the disambiguating patronymic of the *other* Zechariah to this citation. Whether Matthew intended the Jehoiada-era Zechariah (the traditional/majority interpretive view, since the "Abel to Zechariah" pairing tracks Genesis-to-Chronicles as the first-to-last murder in the Hebrew canon's ordering, and since some manuscripts omit the patronymic entirely) or actually meant the son of Berechiah is a matter of real scholarly disagreement, not settled fact. As written, the DB presents the identification as an uncomplicated fact ("Jesus cited his blood") without flagging that the verse's own wording names a different father — which is precisely the kind of same-named-figure disambiguation risk the task brief asked to scrutinize closely.
- **Proposed correction:** Soften the sentence to acknowledge the textual tension, e.g.: "Many interpreters identify him as the Zechariah Jesus refers to among the righteous blood shed from Abel onward (Matt 23:35), though that verse names the victim's father as Berechiah, leading some to think Jesus meant the later prophet Zechariah instead." At minimum, remove the flat assertion "Jesus cited his blood" in favor of language that signals this is an identification, not a certainty.
- **Severity:** Minor — the core narrative (Zechariah son of Jehoiada, stoned in the Temple court, dying words) is accurate and well-supported by 2 Chronicles 24; only the confidently-stated Matt 23:35 cross-reference overstates a genuinely disputed identification.

## Finding 4: Sennacherib's description states he "captured 46 fortified cities" of Judah, but 2 Kings 18:13 (the only ref cited for this person) says only that he took "all the fortified cities of Judah" — the number 46 is not in the biblical text at all; it comes from Sennacherib's own extra-biblical Assyrian annals (the Taylor/Sennacherib Prism)

- **Category:** Unsupported
- **Verse(s):** 2 Kings 18:13 (ESV, live-fetched): "In the fourteenth year of King Hezekiah, Sennacherib king of Assyria came up against all the fortified cities of Judah and took them." No specific count is given anywhere in 2 Kings 18-19 (confirmed by live-fetching the full 18:13–19:9 range and searching specifically for any number). The figure "46" comes from Sennacherib's own royal inscription (the Taylor Prism / Sennacherib's Annals, live-searched): "I besieged and captured forty-six of his fortified cities... taken in battle with my battering rams" — a non-biblical Assyrian source, albeit a widely-cited piece of extra-biblical corroboration for the campaign described in 2 Kings 18.
- **Current DB state:** `scripts/seed-2kings.ts` line 137 (`sennacherib.description`): "King of Assyria who invaded Judah, **captured 46 fortified cities**, and besieged Jerusalem." The only scripture ref for this person (line 273) cites `"2 Kings", 18, 13, 19, 37` — the biblical range that contains the *general* statement ("all the fortified cities") but not the number.
- **Why this is flagged:** This is a borderline case — the number is historically well-attested and not fabricated, and the general fact ("captured Judah's fortified cities") is accurate to 2 Kings 18:13. But the DB states "46" as a plain narrative fact indistinguishable from the surrounding Scripture-sourced details, under a ref that only cites 2 Kings, without any indication that this specific figure is drawn from a different, non-biblical source (Sennacherib's own annals) rather than from the cited biblical text itself. Per the task brief's instruction to include borderline items rather than silently decline them, this is flagged: a reader relying on the cited "2 Kings 18:13–19:37" ref to verify "46" would not find that number anywhere in the passage.
- **Proposed correction:** Either (a) remove the specific number and use the text's own wording, e.g. "invaded Judah, captured its fortified cities, and besieged Jerusalem," or (b) keep the number but attribute it explicitly, e.g. "captured 46 fortified cities per his own Assyrian annals" — consistent with how the DB elsewhere flags Chronicles-sourced additions with attribution (e.g. `manasseh_king.description`: "Chronicles adds that he was taken captive to Babylon...").
- **Severity:** Minor — the figure is real, non-contradictory, historically well-founded, and doesn't misstate what happened (cities were indeed captured); the issue is purely that it's presented as if drawn from the cited Scripture passage when it is not.
