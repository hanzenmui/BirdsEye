## Finding 1: Absalom's hair weight is stated as a bare modern unit ("five pounds") without citing the ESV's actual unit or the "king's weight" qualifier

- **Category:** Incorrect
- **Verse(s):** 2 Samuel 14:26 (ESV, live-fetched via search of biblia.com/ESV.org sourced text): "And when he cut the hair of his head (for at the end of every year he used to cut it, when it was heavy on him, he cut it), he weighed the hair of his head, two hundred shekels by the king's weight."
- **Current DB state:** `scripts/seed-2samuel.ts` line 146, `absalom.description`: "Described as the most handsome man in all Israel — no blemish from head to foot, with hair so thick it weighed **five pounds** when cut annually."
- **Why this is flagged:** The ESV never states "five pounds" anywhere in the text — that figure is a modern-equivalent conversion of "two hundred shekels by the king's weight" that the DB presents as if it were the plain textual fact, with no indication it's a conversion or that the original unit was shekels. This is the same pattern the 1 Samuel audit flagged for Goliath's armor weight (which the 1 Samuel seed file correctly handled by keeping the shekel figure alongside a pound conversion, e.g. "scale armor weighing 125 pounds" — though even there the shekel figure wasn't stated). Here, unlike Goliath's case, the DB drops the ESV's unit entirely and also drops the "by the king's weight" qualifier, which is itself a detail of some interpretive significance (it specifies a particular royal/administrative standard rather than a generic shekel, and its precise value is debated by scholars — estimates for "200 shekels" of hair range roughly 2-5 lbs depending on which shekel standard is assumed, so "five pounds" is on the high end of a genuinely disputed conversion, not a settled one).
- **Proposed correction:** Revise to cite the ESV's actual unit, e.g.: "...with hair so thick it weighed two hundred shekels (by the king's weight) when cut annually — roughly five pounds by common estimates." This preserves the ESV's stated unit as primary while keeping the modern-equivalent gloss for readability.
- **Severity:** Minor — the general point (Absalom's hair was famously thick/heavy) is preserved either way, but the specific number given is not what the ESV states, and "five pounds" is presented with more numeric certainty than the underlying shekel-to-pound conversion actually supports.

## Finding 2: Bathsheba's sons are listed in an order that matches neither 2 Samuel 5:14 nor 1 Chronicles 3:5, and lead with Solomon rather than Shammua/Shimea

- **Category:** Incorrect
- **Verse(s):** 2 Samuel 5:14 (ESV, live-fetched): "And these are the names of those who were born to him in Jerusalem: Shammua, Shobab, Nathan, Solomon." 1 Chronicles 3:5 (ESV, live-fetched): "These were born to him in Jerusalem: Shimea, Shobab, Nathan and Solomon, four by Bath-shua, the daughter of Ammiel."
- **Current DB state:** `scripts/seed-2samuel.ts` line 120, `bathsheba.description`: "She then bore **Solomon, Shimea, Shobab, and Nathan**."
- **Why this is flagged:** Both source texts (2 Sam 5:14, using "Shammua"; 1 Chr 3:5, using the spelling variant "Shimea" — these are the same person, a genuine, well-attested spelling variant, not an error in the DB's choice of "Shimea" itself) list the same order: [Shammua/Shimea], Shobab, Nathan, Solomon — with Solomon named *last* in both lists. The DB reverses this, placing Solomon *first*, followed by Shimea, Shobab, and Nathan in their original relative order. This isn't a trivial detail: Solomon being listed last across two independent genealogical lists is consistent with him being Bathsheba's fourth and final son with David (after the first, unnamed child died per 2 Sam 12:15-19), and the DB's own text elsewhere ("Their first child died... She then bore Solomon, Shimea, Shobab, and Nathan") implies Solomon was born *first* among the surviving four, which contradicts both source texts' consistent ordering.
- **Proposed correction:** Reorder to "She then bore Shimea, Shobab, Nathan, and Solomon" (keeping the DB's existing "Shimea" spelling choice, consistent with 1 Chronicles).
- **Severity:** Important — this isn't just cosmetic ordering; as written it implies a birth sequence that both source texts contradict, and Solomon's position as the last-born (not first-born) of these four sons is a detail with some narrative relevance (he's the youngest survivor, not the heir-apparent by birth order, which factors into why his succession over elder half-brothers like Adonijah in 1 Kings 1 is treated as noteworthy).

## Finding 3: David's second son Chileab (Daniel), born in Hebron to Abigail, is entirely absent from the seed file even though his birth is adjacent to Amnon's and Absalom's, both of which are seeded

- **Category:** Missing
- **Verse(s):** 2 Samuel 3:2-3 (ESV, live-fetched via search): "And sons were born to David at Hebron: his firstborn was Amnon, of Ahinoam of Jezreel; and his second, Chileab, of Abigail the widow of Nabal of Carmel; and the third, Absalom the son of Maacah the daughter of Talmai king of Geshur..." (1 Chronicles 3:1 additionally names him "Daniel.")
- **Current DB state:** No `chileab` (or `daniel`) person record exists anywhere in `scripts/seed-2samuel.ts`, and no relationship references him. `grep -n "chileab\|Daniel" scripts/seed-2samuel.ts` returns no matches.
- **Why this is flagged:** The seed file explicitly enumerates David's Hebron-born sons list contextually (Amnon's description cites "David's firstborn son by Ahinoam of Jezreel," Absalom's description cites "Third son of David, born to Maacah") — i.e., the DB is clearly aware of and using the 2 Sam 3:2-5 birth-order list as its source for these two entries, which makes the silent gap at "second son" conspicuous rather than an incidental omission of a minor figure. That said, I weigh this as a genuine borderline case worth the controller's judgment rather than an open-and-shut correction: Chileab/Daniel has no independent narrative role anywhere in 2 Samuel (he is never mentioned again after the birth-list, unlike every other person in this seed file, all of whom have at least one scene of action), so his omission is defensible under an "active characters only" editorial policy — but if that's the intended policy, Amnon and Absalom's descriptions arguably should not present themselves as complete enumerations of "sons born in Hebron" context without at least a passing acknowledgment that a second son existed. I lean toward this clearing the bar for a finding because a reader cross-referencing Amnon's and Absalom birth-order language ("firstborn," "third son") would reasonably expect the DB to also have a "second son," and its absence could look like an oversight rather than a deliberate scope decision.
- **Proposed correction:** Either (a) add a `chileab` person record (key: `chileab`, name: "Chileab", alsoKnownAs: "Daniel", gender: male, description noting he was David's second son by Abigail, born in Hebron, who — per the near-universal scholarly/traditional inference from his total absence from the rest of the narrative — likely died young, with a `David parent_of Chileab` relationship and a ref to 2 Sam 3:3 / 1 Chr 3:1), or (b) if the controller judges this out of scope for a "named, narratively active persons" policy, no action needed — but I recommend at minimum reviewing this decision explicitly rather than leaving it as a silent gap, since Task 2 (correction script) will need to know whether to add this person or intentionally skip him.
- **Severity:** Minor — Chileab/Daniel has zero narrative action anywhere in Scripture beyond being named at birth, so omitting him doesn't create any inaccuracy in the existing records; this is purely a completeness/coverage question, not a correctness one.

## Finding 4 (borderline, included per brief's instruction to surface uncertain items): Mephibosheth's "Merib-baal" alternate name is never used in 2 Samuel itself — it appears only in 1 Chronicles — while the DB's Ish-bosheth entry's parallel "Esh-baal" name is likewise a Chronicles-only form

- **Category:** Unsupported
- **Verse(s):** 2 Samuel never uses "Merib-baal" (Mephibosheth is named only as "Mephibosheth" throughout, e.g. 2 Sam 4:4, 9:6, 16:1, 19:24) or "Esh-baal" (Ish-bosheth is named only as "Ish-bosheth," e.g. 2 Sam 2:8, 3:8, 4:5). Both baal-forms are attested only in 1 Chronicles 8:33-34 and 9:39-40 (live-searched: "Saul fathered Jonathan, Malchi-shua, Abinadab and Eshbaal" / "...and Jonathan was the father of Merib-baal").
- **Current DB state:** `scripts/seed-2samuel.ts` lines 86-94: both `ish_bosheth.alsoKnownAs = "Esh-baal"` and `mephibosheth.alsoKnownAs = "Merib-baal"`, with `ish_bosheth.description` explicitly framing this as "whose original name was Esh-baal ('man of Baal')" — correctly and accurately describing the well-documented scribal bosheth-for-baal substitution phenomenon (verified via WebSearch: this is mainstream, well-attested text-critical scholarship, not a fringe claim — the same substitution appears in "Jerubbesheth" for "Jerubbaal," Gideon's alternate name, already correctly seeded as `alsoKnownAs: "Jerubbaal"` in `scripts/seed-judges.ts`).
- **Why this is flagged (and why I judge it does *not* clear the bar for a correction):** The underlying scholarly claim (that "bosheth" names are shame-substitutions for genuine, contemporaneous "baal" names) is accurate and correctly framed as a name-substitution rather than a mere "alternate spelling," which is exactly what the task brief asked me to verify. My only hesitation is that the DB's `alsoKnownAs` field doesn't distinguish between "this name appears in 2 Samuel itself" (it doesn't, for either figure) and "this name is attested in Chronicles' genealogies as this person's original/birth name" (it is, for both). Since `alsoKnownAs` is used throughout this seed file (and every other book's seed file, e.g. Gideon/Jerubbaal, Naomi/Mara) as a general "this person is also called X somewhere in Scripture" field rather than a strictly same-book field, and since both instances here are internally consistent with each other (both figures get the same treatment) and accurate to the wider canon, I don't think this needs a correction — I'm flagging it only because the task brief specifically asked me to give special scrutiny to this naming convention, and I want the controller to have visibility into the fact that the "original name" claim rests on Chronicles, not on 2 Samuel's own text.
- **Proposed correction:** No change recommended. If the controller wants additional precision, an option would be appending "(named in 1 Chronicles 8/9, not in 2 Samuel itself)" to one or both descriptions — but no other audited book's findings have added this level of cross-book sourcing caveat to an `alsoKnownAs` field, and doing so here without a consistent policy would be an isolated stylistic change.
- **Severity:** Minor — declined as a formal correction (see reasoning above), included for visibility per audit brief instructions and because the brief flagged this exact naming convention as a special priority item.

## Priority items checked and cleared (no finding)

**(a) Ish-bosheth/Esh-baal and Mephibosheth/Merib-baal dual-naming convention.** Confirmed via WebSearch (multiple independent sources: Britannica, biblehub topical index, Watchtower Insight on Scriptures, armstronginstitute.org, and a peer-reviewed article abstract by Michael Avioz, "The Names Mephibosheth and Ishbosheth Reconsidered") that this is a genuine, well-documented scribal phenomenon: later Hebrew editors systematically substituted "bosheth" (shame) for "baal" (lord/master) in personal names once "Baal" became exclusively associated with the Canaanite storm god, affecting at least three attested cases in this family alone (Eshbaal→Ish-bosheth, Merib-baal→Mephibosheth, and Gideon's son's name Jerub-baal→Jerubbesheth in 2 Sam 11:21). The DB's `ish_bosheth.description` phrase "whose original name was Esh-baal ('man of Baal')" correctly frames this as an actual name substitution, not merely a spelling variant. **No finding — accurate framing.** (See Finding 4 above for a related, but separate, borderline observation about sourcing.)

**(b) The "David sibling_of Zeruiah" relationship's textual basis.** Confirmed 2 Samuel itself never states Zeruiah is David's sister — she is introduced only via her sons ("the three sons of Zeruiah," 2 Sam 2:18) with no genealogical statement connecting her to David. The explicit statement is in 1 Chronicles 2:13-16 (live-fetched): "Jesse fathered Eliab his firstborn, Abinadab the second, Shimea the third... Ozem the sixth, David the seventh. And their sisters were Zeruiah and Abigail." This confirms Zeruiah as a full sister of David (both children of Jesse). Checked the DB's actual relationship note at `scripts/seed-2samuel.ts` line 187 — `insertRelByName("David", "sibling_of", "Zeruiah", "Zeruiah is David's sister (1 Chr 2:16)")` — and it **already cites 1 Chronicles 2:16, not a 2 Samuel verse.** This is correctly sourced as-is. **No finding — the citation already points to the correct verse.** (Note as an aside, not a finding: 2 Samuel 17:25 separately calls Zeruiah's sister Abigail "the daughter of Nahash," which conflicts with the Jesse-parentage implied by 1 Chr 2:16 and is a recognized textual crux among commentators — but this doesn't affect the DB's Zeruiah/David relationship, which is soundly based on 1 Chr 2:16 alone.)

**(c) Bathsheba/David/Uriah/Nathan sequence.** Verified the full narrative chain against live-fetched/searched text: David sees Bathsheba bathing and sends for her (2 Sam 11:2-4); Uriah is recalled from battle and refuses to go home to his wife while the ark and army are in the field (11:6-11, confirmed via search); David has Joab position Uriah at the front and withdraw support, and Uriah is killed (11:14-17); David marries Bathsheba (11:27); Nathan confronts David with the parable of the ewe lamb and says "You are the man" (12:1-7, confirmed via search); the first child dies (12:15-19); Solomon is born and named, and Nathan gives him the name Jedidiah (12:24-25, confirmed via live search: "the LORD loved him and sent word by Nathan the prophet, so he called his name Jedidiah, because of the LORD"). All matches the DB's descriptions for `bathsheba`, `uriah`, `nathan`, and `david` exactly. **No finding.**

**(d) Absalom's hair-weight description.** See Finding 1 above — this did clear the bar for a finding.

## Other details checked (no finding)

- **Zeruiah's description "David's sister (or half-sister)" hedge (line 99).** This hedge is defensible: 1 Chr 2:16 lists her among "their sisters" following the list of Jesse's seven sons, which most naturally reads as full siblingship, but some genealogical harmonization discussions (checked via the same search results reviewed for Finding 4/priority item (b)) raise the 2 Sam 17:25 "daughter of Nahash" question as a reason some propose half-sibling status (e.g., a theory that Nahash was Jesse's wife's first husband or an alternate name for Jesse). The DB's parenthetical hedge accurately reflects this genuine ambiguity rather than overstating certainty. Not flagged.
- **Abner's death "in revenge for his brother Asahel" (joab.description, line 104) and the `joab enemy_of abner` relationship note (line 191).** Matches 2 Sam 3:27, live-fetched: "he struck him there in the stomach, so that he died, for the blood of Asahel his brother." Accurate.
- **Joab's fate — "Executed by Solomon at David's deathbed request" (line 104).** This is from 1 Kings 2:5-6, 28-34, outside 2 Samuel's scope but consistent with the forward-looking-biography convention already established across prior books' audits (e.g., 1 Samuel's Merab entry citing 2 Sam 21:8-9). Not flagged.
- **Ahithophel's motive — "possibly because Bathsheba was his granddaughter and Uriah his kinsman" (line 152).** Verified this is correctly hedged as speculative ("possibly"): 2 Sam 11:3 names Bathsheba's father as "Eliam," and 2 Sam 23:34 (in the list of David's mighty men) names "Eliam the son of Ahithophel" — if these are the same Eliam, Ahithophel is Bathsheba's grandfather, but this identification, while widely repeated, is not textually certain (the two Eliams could be different people, and no verse explicitly states the connection). The DB's "possibly" hedge is appropriately cautious and matches the actual state of the evidence. Not flagged. Uriah's status as Ahithophel's "kinsman" is a looser, more speculative inference (from Uriah's inclusion in the same mighty-men list near Eliam, 2 Sam 23:34, 39) which the DB also appropriately hedges with "possibly." Not flagged.
