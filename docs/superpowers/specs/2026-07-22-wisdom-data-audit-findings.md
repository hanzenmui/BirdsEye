## Finding 1: Agur's description quotes Proverbs 30:1 with wording that is not the ESV's — the actual ESV text says something substantively different

- **Category:** Incorrect
- **Verse(s):** Proverbs 30:1-3 (ESV, live-fetched, verbatim, cross-confirmed via two independent fetches): "The words of Agur son of Jakeh. The oracle. The man declares, I am weary, O God; I am weary, O God, and worn out. Surely I am too stupid to be a man. I have not the understanding of a man. I have not learned wisdom, nor have I knowledge of the Holy One."
- **Current DB state:** `scripts/seed-wisdom.ts` line 81, `agur.description`: "Sage who authored Proverbs 30. Opens with profound humility: **'I am the most ignorant of men; I do not have human wisdom.'** His oracle contains numerical proverbs about things that are never satisfied. Identity uncertain; some traditions link him to Solomon."
- **Why this is flagged:** The description presents this as a direct quotation ("Opens with... :") of Proverbs 30:1, but the ESV's actual text of 30:1-3 is "I am weary, O God... Surely I am too stupid to be a man. I have not the understanding of a man. I have not learned wisdom, nor have I knowledge of the Holy One." The quoted phrase "I am the most ignorant of men; I do not have human wisdom" does not appear in the ESV anywhere in this passage — it is close in sense to other translations' renderings (e.g., NIV-style phrasing) of vv. 2-3, but is not ESV wording, and this codebase's stated source-of-truth convention for this audit series is the ESV. The underlying theme (Agur's self-deprecating humility about his lack of wisdom) is accurately characterized in substance, but the field presents a specific misquotation as if verbatim.
- **Proposed correction:** Replace the quoted sentence with the actual ESV wording or a non-quotation paraphrase, e.g.: "Opens with profound humility, declaring himself 'too stupid to be a man' who has 'not learned wisdom, nor... knowledge of the Holy One' (Prov 30:2-3)." His oracle contains numerical proverbs about things that are never satisfied. Identity uncertain; some traditions link him to Solomon.
- **Severity:** Important — a quoted scripture string that does not match the source text is a specific, verifiable factual error, but it is a single self-contained description-field correction with no cascading structural effect, consistent with this series' precedent of reserving Critical for structural gaps.

## Finding 2: The Shulamite's name and alsoKnownAs fields spell her title "Shulamite" (one m); the ESV spells it "Shulammite" (two m's)

- **Category:** Incorrect
- **Verse(s):** Song of Solomon 6:13 (ESV, live-fetched, verbatim, cross-confirmed via two independent fetches): "Return, return, O **Shulammite**, return, return, that we may look upon you. Why should you look upon the **Shulammite**, as upon a dance before two armies?"
- **Current DB state:** `scripts/seed-wisdom.ts` lines 106-111, `shulamite.name`: "Shulamite"; `shulamite.alsoKnownAs`: "the beloved, the Shulamite woman"; description also uses "Shulamite" and "possibly from Shunem."
- **Why this is flagged:** The ESV's own text of the only verse where this title occurs (6:13, both instances) spells it "Shulammite" with a double m. The DB's `name` field and `alsoKnownAs` field both use the single-m "Shulamite" spelling throughout. This spelling does appear in some other translations (e.g., KJV) and in general reference works, so it is not an invented or unrecognizable form, but per this series' ESV-as-source-of-truth convention, the DB's primary `name` field should match the ESV's actual spelling of the term it is directly quoting/deriving from.
- **Proposed correction:** Change `name` from "Shulamite" to "Shulammite," and update `alsoKnownAs` to "the beloved, the Shulammite woman" (optionally retaining "Shulamite" as a secondary alternate spelling in the AKA field, since it is a recognized variant in other translations). Also update the description's two occurrences of "Shulamite" to "Shulammite" for internal consistency.
- **Severity:** Important — a proper-name spelling mismatched against the cited source text's own spelling of that exact word; single-field correction, not a structural gap.

Both missing verses (3:5, 8:4) are confirmed via live ESV fetch to contain the same "do not stir up or awaken love" refrain addressed to the Daughters of Jerusalem, textually identical in kind to the already-ref'd 2:7. This is exactly the kind of gap the task brief flagged for specific attention.
- **Proposed correction:** Add two new `scripture_refs` rows for `daughters_jerusalem`: Song of Solomon 3:5 (note: "Do not stir up love until it pleases (refrain, repeated from 2:7)") and Song of Solomon 8:4 (note: "Do not stir up love until it pleases (final refrain)").
- **Severity:** Important — this is a coverage gap (missing refs) consistent with the description's own stated citation list, not a structural/relationship gap; scoped to two new ref rows on an existing person with no cascading effect on other records.

## Finding 4 (borderline — included per brief's instruction to flag rather than silently omit): `shulamite spouse_of solomon` asserts a settled marital relationship the Song's own text does not explicitly state in the Shulamite's own words, and which is genuinely contested in scholarship

- **Category:** Unsupported
- **Verse(s):** Song of Solomon 1:1 (ESV, verbatim): "The Song of Songs, which is Solomon's." (Superscription — an authorship/dedication attribution, not a statement that the woman in the poem is married to Solomon.) Song of Solomon 3:11 (ESV, verbatim): "Go out, O daughters of Zion, and look upon King Solomon, with the crown with which his mother crowned him on the day of his wedding, on the day of the gladness of his heart." (Third-person narrative interlude describing Solomon's wedding day — does not identify the bride as the Shulamite by name, nor is it in the woman's own voice.) Throughout the book, the woman's own first-person address to her lover consistently uses "my beloved" (e.g., Song 2:16, 5:16, 6:3) rather than naming him Solomon or calling him "husband"/"my king."
- **Current DB state:** `scripts/seed-wisdom.ts` line 133: `insertRel("shulamite", "spouse_of", "solomon", "The Song of Solomon is framed as a love song between the Shulamite and Solomon")`.
- **Why this is flagged:** Standard scholarly discussion (researched live, not from memory) identifies a genuine, long-running debate over whether the book's two lovers are Solomon and the Shulamite (traditional two-character reading) or whether Solomon is a third character — a king attempting to win the Shulamite away from a shepherd she truly loves (the "shepherd hypothesis" / three-character interpretation, associated with 19th-century scholar Ewald and still discussed in modern commentaries, partly motivated by the tension between the woman's singular devotion and Solomon's harem of "sixty queens and eighty concubines" referenced in 6:8-9). The 1:1 superscription is an authorship/dedication note, not a claim that the woman is Solomon's wife; the woman never once names her lover "Solomon" in her own speech. The DB's note text ("framed as...") is itself appropriately hedged, and the traditional reading (with 3:11's wedding-day language) does give real textual support to treating Solomon as the bridegroom — so this is not a clear-cut error, but the relationship type `spouse_of` asserts a firm, unqualified relationship in the DB's structured data despite the underlying identification being genuinely disputed in a way the `type` field itself cannot express (unlike the note text, which does hedge).
- **Proposed correction:** No clear consensus correction is proposed. Two reasonable options for Task 2/3 to weigh: (a) leave as-is, since the traditional two-character reading with 3:11's wedding language is the majority/traditional view and the note already hedges with "framed as"; or (b) retype as `"other"` (matching this file's own precedent for the similarly-hedged `agur`/`lemuel` Solomon-identity relationships) to better reflect that the pairing is a literary/traditional framing rather than an asserted marital fact, while keeping the same note text. Flagging for controller judgment rather than asserting a specific required change.
- **Severity:** Minor — this is an interpretive/typing question about a relationship whose textual basis is genuinely debated in mainstream scholarship, not a clear factual error; included per the brief's instruction to surface borderline items rather than silently omit them.
