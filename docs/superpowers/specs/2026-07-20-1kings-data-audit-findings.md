## Finding 1: Obadiah's hiding place for the hundred prophets is stated as "caves" (plural) where the ESV consistently says "a cave" (singular)

- **Category:** Incorrect
- **Verse(s):** 1 Kings 18:4 (ESV, live-fetched/searched): "and when Jezebel cut off the prophets of the LORD, Obadiah took a hundred prophets and hid them by fifties in a cave and fed them with bread and water." 1 Kings 18:13 (ESV, live-fetched/searched, Obadiah's own recounting to Elijah): "how I hid a hundred men of the LORD's prophets by fifties in a cave and fed them with bread and water?" Both verses independently use the singular "a cave."
- **Current DB state:** `scripts/seed-1kings.ts` line 143, `obadiah_steward.description`: "When Jezebel was killing the prophets, he hid a hundred of them **in caves** and supplied them with bread and water."
- **Why this is flagged:** The task brief specifically called out this exact detail ("Obadiah hid the prophets 'by fifties in a cave,' totaling roughly 100 — check the seed file's exact wording against this") as a priority numeric/physical detail to verify with special care, in the same category as prior books' Achan gold weight, Eli's ref range, and Absalom's hair weight errors. The ESV text is unambiguous and consistent across both of its two independent statements of this detail (18:4 and 18:13): the prophets were hidden "by fifties in **a** cave" — i.e., two groups of fifty sharing a single cave (or, per some commentators, one cave used for the operation), not multiple caves. The DB's plural "caves" changes this specific, twice-attested detail. (Note: `jezebel.description` at line 138 states the same fact without specifying cave count at all — "Obadiah hid a hundred of them" — so only the `obadiah_steward` record has this error.)
- **Proposed correction:** Change "he hid a hundred of them in caves" to "he hid a hundred of them in a cave" (or "hid them fifty to a cave," but the simplest fix preserving the DB's existing sentence structure is singular "a cave," matching the ESV's own repeated wording).
- **Severity:** Minor — the substantive facts (Obadiah hid ~100 prophets from Jezebel, in groups of fifty, supplying bread and water) are all otherwise correct and match the text; only the singular/plural of "cave" is off, and it doesn't materially change the reader's understanding of the episode.

**(l) Rehoboam's mother Naamah the Ammonite (1 Kgs 14:21).** ESV (live-searched): "his mother's name was Naamah the Ammonite." DB (`rehoboam.description`, line 111): "Son of Solomon by Naamah the Ammonite." Exact match. No finding.

**(m) Solomon's judgment of the two mothers, wisdom request at Gibeon, and 30,000 conscripted laborers.** ESV confirms Solomon's request for wisdom at Gibeon (1 Kgs 3:5-12), the two-mothers judgment (3:16-28), and the levy of 30,000 men for Lebanon (5:13-14). DB matches all three ("At Gibeon God offered him anything; he asked for wisdom. His judgment with the two mothers... supervised 30,000 conscripted laborers"). No finding.

**(n) Jeroboam's identity as "an Ephraimite official under Solomon."** ESV (1 Kgs 11:26, live-searched): "Jeroboam the son of Nebat, an Ephraimite of Zeredah, a servant of Solomon." DB (`jeroboam.description`, line 117): "Son of Nebat, an Ephraimite official under Solomon." "Official" vs. the ESV's "servant" is a loose paraphrase (Jeroboam is elsewhere described in the text as being put "in charge of all the forced labor of the house of Joseph," 1 Kgs 11:28, which does support an "official" characterization), not a contradiction. Not flagged — this is interpretive shorthand consistent with the actual supervisory role given to him in the same chapter, not an invented detail.

**(o) Ben-hadad's two sieges, the treaty, and the "devoted to destruction" prophetic rebuke.** ESV confirms: first siege and Ahab's initial agreement then refusal of the second demand (20:1-12); second siege at Aphek the following spring and Israel's victory (20:26-30); Ben-hadad's appeal for mercy and Ahab's treaty (20:31-34); the prophet's rebuke that Ahab let go "the man I had devoted to destruction" and that his life would go for Ben-hadad's (20:35-42). DB (`ben_hadad.description`, line 153) matches this sequence closely, including "this spared life that God had devoted to destruction, costing Ahab his own life in the prophetic rebuke that followed." No finding.

**(p) Ahab's death and the dogs-licking-blood detail (1 Kgs 22:34-38).** ESV confirms Ahab was struck by "a certain man" drawing his bow "at random" (22:34) and died that evening at Ramoth-gilead; his chariot was later washed at the pool of Samaria, "and the dogs licked up his blood... according to the word of the LORD" (22:38). DB (`ahab.description`, line 134): "Killed by a random arrow at Ramoth-gilead; his blood was licked by dogs as Elijah had prophesied." This is accurate as written — the DB doesn't claim the dogs licked his blood *at* Ramoth-gilead (only that he was killed there), and the blood-licking-by-dogs detail is correctly attributed to fulfillment of Elijah's prophecy per 22:38's own text ("according to the word of the LORD"). (Aside, not a finding: there is a well-known interpretive crux around 1 Kgs 21:19's specific wording that Ahab's blood would be licked "in the place where dogs licked up the blood of Naboth" — i.e., at the vineyard in Jezreel — which most commentators reconcile via Ahab's repentance (21:27-29) deferring that specific place-detail to his son Joram's death in 2 Kgs 9:25-26, while the *general* prophecy of dogs licking Ahab's own blood is separately and directly fulfilled at Samaria per 22:38. The DB's phrasing doesn't engage this crux and isn't contradicted by it, since it doesn't specify a location for the blood-licking.) No finding.

**(q) Jezebel's death by dogs (2 Kgs 9:33-36), cited in the 1 Kings-scoped `jezebel.description`.** This event is outside 1 Kings' own text (it occurs in 2 Kings 9), but it fulfills a prophecy stated within 1 Kings itself (21:23, live-searched: "The dogs shall eat Jezebel within the walls of Jezreel"), and the DB's forward-looking-biography convention (established across all prior books' audits) treats this as acceptable. ESV (2 Kgs 9:33, 9:35, live-searched) confirms Jezebel was thrown from a window at Jehu's command and her corpse was eaten by dogs, leaving only her skull, feet, and palms. DB: "Died when thrown from a window by her own servants at Jehu's command; her corpse was eaten by dogs as Elijah had prophesied." Accurate — "her own servants" matches 2 Kgs 9:32-33 (the eunuchs in her chamber threw her down at Jehu's order). No finding.
